# Tax residency status examples — Analysis for 2023 … Analysis for 2025

Part 2 of 4 of Tax residency status examples (source document irs-tax-residency-status-examples). Section: Analysis for 2023 … Analysis for 2025.

#### Analysis for 2023

Date of entry into United States: 03-15-2023
Nonexempt individual 03-15-2023 through 11-14-2023
Exempt individual 11-15-2023 through 12-31-2027
 (As a student on an F-1 visa, if R continues to substantially comply with the requirements of the visa, R will remain an “exempt individual” for 5 calendar years, 2023 through 2027.)
Begin counting days on 03-15-2023
Stop counting days on 11-14-2023

Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2023: 245 days
Number of exempt days in United States during 2023: 47 days

Count days as follows:

Current year (2023) days in United States (245) × 1 = 245 days
Prior year (2022) days in United States (0) × 1/3 = 0 days
Year before that (2021) days in United States (0) × 1/6 = 0 days
Total for 2023 = 245 days

R met the substantial presence test on 09-13-2023 (the 183rd day after arriving in the United States). R's residency starting date under IRC § 7701(b) is 03-15-2023 (first day of presence in the United States during the calendar year in which R met the substantial presence test).

Under the general rule, the residency ending date under the substantial presence test is December 31st of the year in which the individual ceases to be present in the United States. However, an exception is allowed for a residency ending date that is earlier than December 31st of a non-U.S. citizen’s last calendar year in the United States. Refer to Residency Starting and Ending Dates.

The exception allows one’s residency ending date to be the last day during the calendar year in which they cease to be present in the United States if, for the remainder of the calendar year:

1. their tax home is in a foreign country (Rev. Rul. 93-86); and
2. they maintain a closer connection to that foreign country than to the United States (Treas. Reg. § 301.7701(b)-2(d)).

If R had qualified for the exception, R's residency ending date under the exception to the general rule would have been 11-14-2023.

However, R did not qualify for the exception to the general rule for determining one’s residency ending date because, after 11-14-2023, R did not have a tax home in a foreign country and did not maintain a closer connection to a foreign country than to the United States.
Since R does not qualify for the exception to the residency ending date general rule, R’s residency ending date is 12-31-2023.

#### Analysis for 2024

Date of entry into United States: 03-15-2023
Nonexempt individual: 03-15-2023 through 11-14-2023
Exempt individual: 11-15-2023 through 12-31-2027 (provided R substantially complies with the requirements of the F-1 student visa)
Begin counting days on 03-15-2023
Stop counting days on 11-14-2023

Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2024: 0 days
Number of exempt days in United States 365 days

Count days as follows:

Current year (2024) days in United States (0) × 1 = 0 days
Prior year (2023) days in United States (245 nonexempt days) × 1/3 = 81 2/3 days
Year before that (2022) days in United States (0) × 1/6 = 0 days
Total for 2024 = 81 days

R did not meet the substantial presence test for 2024. Per the analysis for 2023 above, R's residency beginning date was 03-15-2023 and R’s residency ending date, under the general rule, was 12-31-2023. Since the substantial presence test is applied on a year-to-year basis, R is a nonresident for calendar year 2024.

## Example 7

K was a citizen and resident of a foreign country just prior to arriving in the United States. K had never been to the United States prior to arriving on 08-25-2023 as a student on an F-1 visa. On 02-02-2024 K married a United States citizen. On 03-01-2024 K petitioned USCIS for a change in immigration status to that of lawful permanent resident based upon the marriage. On 05-15-2025 USCIS approved K’s petition to become a lawful permanent resident of the United States and issued K a green card later in the year under the routine procedures of the USCIS. Determine K’s residency starting date.

### Solution:

#### Analysis for 2023

Date of entry into United States: 08-25-2023
Exempt individual: 08-25-2023 through 05-14-2025
Begin counting days on 05-15-2025
Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2023: 0 days
Number of exempt days in United States during 2023: 129 days (08-25-2023 through 12-31-2023)

Count days as follows:

Current year (2023) days in United States (0) × 1 = 0 days
Prior year (2022) days in United States (0) × 1/3 = 0 days
Year before that (2021) days in United States (0) × 1/6 = 0 days
Total for 2023 = 0 days

K does not meet the substantial presence test in 2023.

#### Analysis for 2024

Date of entry into United States: 08-25-2023
Exempt individual: 08-25-2023 through 05-14-2025
Begin counting days on 05-15-2025
Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2024: 0 days
Number of exempt days in United States during 2024: 365 days

Count days as follows:

Current year (2024) days in United States (0) × 1 = 0 days
Prior year (2023) days in United States (0) × 1/3 = 0 days
Year before that (2022) days in United States (0) × 1/6 = 0 days
Total for 2024 = 0 days

K does not meet the substantial presence test for 2024 and K does not meet the green card test for 2024. The filing of a petition with USCIS does not constitute a change of status under immigration law.

#### Analysis for 2025

Date of entry into United States: 08-25-2023
Exempt individual: 08-25-2023 through 05-14-2025
Begin counting days on 05-15-2025.
Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2025: 231 days (05-15-2025 through 12-31-2025)
Number of exempt days in United States during 2025: 134 days (01-01-2025 through 05-14-2025)

Count days as follows:

Current year (2025) days in United States (231) × 1 = 231 days
Prior year (2024) days in United States (0) × 1/3 = 0 days
Year before that (2023) days in United States (0) × 1/6 = 0 days
Total for 2025 = 231 days

K met the substantial presence test on 11-13-2025 (the 183rd day after 05-14-2025). K met the green card test on 05-15-2025.

Since K was already in the United States when K became a lawful permanent resident (green card test), K’s residency starting date under IRC § 7701(b) is 05-15-2025, per both the green card test (the date USCIS changed K’s status to lawful permanent resident) and the substantial presence test (the first day of presence in the United States during the calendar year in which K met the substantial presence test). An "exempt individual" is never counted as being physically present in the United States for purposes of the substantial presence test.

What kind of federal income tax returns will K file for 2023, 2024, and 2025?

2023:

K will file Form 1040-NR as a nonresident.

2024:

Option # 1. K will file Form 1040-NR as a nonresident, married filing separately.

Option # 2. K will file a joint 1040 with K’s United States citizen spouse (they were married on 02-02-2024), making an election under IRC § 6013(g), Election to Treat Nonresident Alien Individual as Resident of the United States. If so, K must file as a U.S. resident in subsequent years.

2025:

Option # 1. If K did not file jointly for 2024 (Option 2, above) K can file as a dual-status individual (Form 1040 with Form 1040-NR attached as a schedule), married filing separately, as K became a lawful permanent resident on 05-15-2025.

Option # 2. K can file Form 1040, either separately or jointly with K’s U.S. citizen spouse.
