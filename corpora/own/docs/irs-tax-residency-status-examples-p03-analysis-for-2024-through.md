# Tax residency status examples — Analysis for 2024

Part 3 of 4 of Tax residency status examples (source document irs-tax-residency-status-examples). Section: Analysis for 2024.

#### Analysis for 2024

Nonexempt individual (individuals in H-1b status are never exempt individuals)
Date of entry into United States: 08-15-2024
Begin counting days on 08-15-2024
Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2024: 139 days (08-15-2024 through 12-31-2024)

Count days as follows:

Current year (2024) days in United States (139) × 1 = 139 days
Prior year (2023) days in United States (0) × 1/3 = 0 days
Year before that (2022) days in United States (0) × 1/6 = 0 days
Total = 139 days

S does not meet the substantial presence test during 2024.

As another option, S could make the first-year choice under IRC § 7701(b)(2)(A) and qualify for a residency starting date of 08-15-2024 because S did not meet either the green card or substantial presence test in the prior year (2023), was present in the United States for at least 31 days in a row in 2024, was present in the United States for at least 75% of the number of days beginning with the first day of the 31-day period (08-15-2024) and ending with the last day of 2024, and met the substantial presence test in the subsequent year (2025).

#### Analysis for 2025

Nonexempt individual (individuals in H-1b status are never exempt individuals)
Date of entry into United States: 08-15-2024
Begin counting days on 08-15-2024
Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2025: 365 days

Count days as follows:

Current year (2025) days in United States (365) × 1 = 365 days
Prior year (2024) days in United States (139) × 1/3 = 46 1/3 days
Year before that (2023) days in United States (0) × 1/6 = 0 days
Total = 411 days

S met the substantial presence test on 05-17-2025.  From 2024, 46 days, plus 137 days from 2025 (01-01-2025 through 05-17-2025) = 183 days.

S's residency starting date is 01-01-2025 (the first day of presence in United States during the calendar year in which S met the substantial presence test).

What kind of federal income tax returns will S file for 2024 and 2025?

2024:

Option #1. S will file Form 1040-NR as a nonresident.

Option #2. S will file a dual-status tax return as a dual-status individual, making the first-year choice under IRC § 7701(b)(2)(A) with a residency starting date of 08-15-2024.

2025:

S will file Form 1040 as a U.S. resident.

## Example 9

D was a citizen and resident of a foreign country just prior to arriving in the United States. D arrived in the United States for the first time ever on 08-15-2018 as a student on an F-1 visa. D remained in F-1 status until graduating in June 2023. D left the United States on 06-30-2023 and returned home. On 08-01-2024 D returned to the United States as a researcher on a J-1 visa. Determine D’s residency starting date for the most recent visit.

### Solution:

#### Analysis for 2024

Date of (second) entry into United States: 08-01-2024
Exempt individual: 08-15-2018 through 12-31-2022
Nonexempt individual: 01-01-2023 through 06-30-2023 and 08-01-2024 through 12-31-2024

Begin counting days of presence in the United States on 08-01-2024. Because D is currently a J-1 non-student, apply the 6-year “lookback rule”. Because D had already been an exempt individual as an F-1 student during 2 of the 6 years prior to 2024, D cannot be an exempt individual during 2024 and must start counting days of presence on the date of arrival in the United States. During D’s prior visit as an F-1 student, applying the 5-year rule, D ceased to be an exempt individual on 12-31-2022.

Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2024: 153 days (08-01-2024 through 12-31-2024)

Count days as follows:

Current year (2024) days in United States (153) × 1 = 153 days
Prior year (2023) days in United States (181 for 01-01-2023 through 06-30-2023) × 1/3 = 60 1/3 days
Year before that (2022) days in United States (0) × 1/6 = 0 days
Total = 213 days

D met the substantial presence test on 12-01-2024.  From 2023, 60 1/3 days, plus 123 days from 2024 (08-01-2024 through 12-01-2024) = 183 days.

D's residency starting date is 08-01-2024. D had not been present in the United States long enough to establish residency during 2023, nor to qualify for the first-year choice under IRC § 7701(b)(2)(A) – D was present in the United States for less than 75% of the days from 01-01-2023 (first day present in the United States as a nonexempt individual) to the last day of 2023. Thus, D’s residency must begin on 08-01-2024, the first day present in the United States during the year D met the substantial presence test.

#### Analysis for 2025

Date of entry into United States: 08-01-2024
Exempt individual: 08-15-2018 through 12-31-2022 (5 calendar years, 2018 through 2022)
Nonexempt individual 01-01-2023 through 06-30-2023 and 08-01-2024 through 12-31-2024
Begin counting days of presence in the United States on 08-01-2024
Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2025: 365 days

Count days as follows:

Current year (2025) days in United States (365) × 1 = 365 days
Prior year (2024) days in United States (153) × 1/3 = 51 days
Year before that (2023) days in United States (181) × 1/6 = 30 1/6 days
Total = 446 days

D met the substantial presence test for 2025.

Since D was a U.S. resident on 12-31-2024, and has not left the United States, D’s residency in the United States established in 2024 continues into 2025 with no residency ending date.

What kind of federal income tax returns will D file for 2024 and 2025?

2024:

D is a dual-status individual and will file a dual-status income tax return.

2025:

D will file Form 1040 as a U.S. resident.

## Example 10

M was a citizen and resident of a foreign country who had never been in the United States prior to arriving 08-15-2022 as a researcher on a J-1 visa. Without leaving the United States, M became a student and changed to F-1 status on 08-10-2024. Determine M’s residency starting date.

### Solution:

#### Analysis for 2024

Date of entry into United States: 08-15-2022
Exempt individual: 2022 and 2023 (researcher on J-1 visa is an exempt individual for 2 calendar years with a 6-year “lookback rule”)
Nonexempt individual: 01-01-2024 through 08-09-2024

Begin counting days on 01-01-2024. However, days as an exempt individual start again on 08-10-2024. Since M became an F-1 student on 08-10-2024, the 6-year “lookback rule” does not apply to M after that date.

Since M has already been an exempt individual during 2022 and 2023, M’s status as a student exempt individual can only continue through 2024, 2025, and 2026. As a student in F-1 status, M is an exempt individual for 5 calendar years, 2022 through 2026.

During 2024 M’s status as an exempt individual begins on 08-10-2024. Therefore, the number of nonexempt days in the United States during 2024 is 221 days.
Number of nonexempt days in United States during 2024: 221 days (01-01-2024 through 08-09-2024)

Count days as follows:

Current year (2024) days in United States (221) × 1 = 221 days
Prior year (2023) days in United States (0) × 1/3 = 0 days
Year before that (2022) days in United States (0) × 1/6 = 0 days
Total = 221 days

M met the substantial presence test on 07-02-2024 (the 183rd day after 12-31-2023).

M's residency starting date is 01-01-2024 (first day present in the United States during the year M met the substantial presence test).

Under the general rule, the residency ending date under the substantial presence test is December 31st of the year in which one ceases to be present in the United States.

However, an exception is allowed for a residency ending date that is earlier than December 31st of a non-U.S. citizen’s last calendar year in the United States. Refer to Residency Starting and Ending Dates.

The exception allows M’s residency ending date to be the last day during the calendar year that M ceases to be present in the United States if, for the remainder of the calendar year:

1. M’s tax home is in a foreign country (Rev. Rul. 93-86); and
2. M maintains a closer connection to that foreign country than to the United States (Treas. Reg. § 301.7701(b)-2(d)).

If M had qualified for the exception, M’s residency ending date under the exception to the general rule would have been 08-09-2024 (the date M ceased to be considered present in the United States, since M became an exempt individual on that date).

However, M did not qualify for the exception to the general rule for determining one’s residency ending date because, after 08-09-2024, M did not have a tax home in a foreign country and did not maintain a closer connection to a foreign country than to the United States.

Since M did not qualify for the exception to the residency ending date general rule, M’s residency ending date was 12-31-2024.
